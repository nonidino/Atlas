The single-cell figure is a worst case over the whole field and will always be much larger than the difference in farm power, which is what the turbines actually integrate.

What this window is for

This is half of the demonstration. This half asks: for one layout, does the coupled graph give the same answer as the undivided classical solver, and what does each cost? The difference between the two power numbers is the price of the cut — the same equations, the same grid, the same viscosity, split up and re-assembled — and not a disagreement about physics.

The other half is on the main screen, and the classical solver cannot do it. Optimising a layout needs the derivative of farm power with respect to every turbine position and angle at once. The coupled graph is built out of differentiable operations end to end, so that derivative comes back from one extra pass. The classical solver here is a black box that returns a number: to search with it you would have to re-run it once per perturbed layout, which is hundreds of runs of the cost shown above instead of tens.

So read the timings honestly. If the classical column is the faster one, that is a real result and it is on screen: splitting a domain into windows and re-assembling them is not free, and at these sizes it does not pay for itself in forward speed. What it buys is the gradient.
