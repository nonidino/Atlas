A composed graph of frozen physics experts — faster than the undivided solver, and differentiable

The same wind farm is solved two ways. On the left, the domain is cut into overlapping windows, each marched by its own copy of a frozen fluid expert and re-assembled every step. On the right, one undivided classical solver over the whole domain — the referent, and the thing that decides who is right. Every number on this page was measured on the machine you are running it on, with the two columns taking turns so that neither is timed against the other's load.

The single-cell figure is a worst case over the whole field and is always much larger than the difference in farm power, which is what the turbines actually integrate and what the optimiser maximises. Both are shown because they answer different questions.

These two power numbers come from the measurement: every column marched from still air for the same number of steps. The big number lower down comes from the live march, which is warm-started and carries the history of every layout you have dragged through, so it is a different estimator and will not agree with them.
