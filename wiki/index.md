| ★★★ **Port algebra** *(binding)* | [[port-algebra-atlas-0.1]] | **The interface contract, closed and rebuilt 2026-08-19.** Retires the accreted vocabulary (`heat`/`pressure`/`stress`/`fluid`/`momentum`/`mass`/`shear`/`conservation`) for **five effort–flow ports whose product is power** — `MECH` $(\boldsymbol\sigma\!\cdot\!\mathbf n,\mathbf v)$, `ROT` $(\tau,\omega)$, `THERM` $(T,q_n/T)$, `ELEC` $(\phi,\mathbf j\!\cdot\!\mathbf n)$, and the `ADVEC` multiport $(\dot m; h_0, Y_k)$. **`MECH` absorbs four old labels exactly** (pressure and stress are the isotropic and deviatoric parts of one tensor; shear is its tangential component). **`conservation` was never a quantity** — it is the definition of a port, so it becomes a residual reported everywhere plus a `conservative` flag. | ★★ **Symmetry averaging** *(first exact enforcement)* | [[symmetry-averaging-atlas-0.1]] | **The composition layer supplying an invariance the frozen expert provably lacks — exactly, without training.** The Reynolds average $\tilde E(\mathbf u)=\lvert G\rvert ^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}g^{-1}E(g\mathbf u)$ is $G$-equivariant for *any* operator $E$, learned or hostile, at a cost of one forward pass per group element; the proof turns on $G$ being **closed**, and averaging over a mere set gives a smoothing that is not equivariant and does not say so, which is why closure is checked numerically at construction. Built for **OP-6**: gate W7's mirror residual goes from $2.77\times10^{-2}$ and **growing** to $2.90\times10^{-7}$ and **flat**, under the $10^{-6}$ gate, for $1.7\times$ the wall time. Isolated, the averaged operator is symmetric **bitwise**, not to machine precision — the reflection is an exact array reversal and floating-point addition is commutative. Averaging is linear, so C1 and the mean survive it untouched; what it cannot do is make the expert *correct*, only *equivariant*. Contrast **OP-1**, whose projection failed because a chosen correction direction was degenerate: an average over a group cannot be, because it chooses no direction |
| ★★ **Schwarz iteration** *(built 2026-08-23; S1 refused as unbuildable)* | [[schwarz-iteration-atlas-0.1]] | **Buying a frozen expert an effective field of view larger than its input shape, in forward passes rather than parameters.** The planned answer to **OP-5**'s one-pass-exchange candidate — and the page argues its own fix is the *minor* term. Three separations do the work. **(1)** “Schwarz” already means the R1 *halo* variant here, tested at W3 and not adopted; R1-vs-R2 asks **what** crosses an interface, this asks **how many times**, and the adopted R2 contract needs no change. **(2)** The machinery already exists — the §8.3 thrust fixed point re-runs the fluid sweep $\sim3\times$ per macro-step and is stopped by a **scalar** test on one rotor, so what is missing is the *convergence criterion*, not the loop. **(3)** The load-bearing theorem: a Schwarz fixed point is the global solution **only if each local solve is a faithful restriction** of it — and **a periodic window is not**, so iterating the present build converges to the fixed point of the wrong map, more self-consistently, with the interface residual (the only symptom) removed. That **reorders OP-5's candidates**: per-window periodicity is a *precondition* of iteration helping, not a peer of it. Predicts the effect is small at tile seams and marginal-but-visible at declared ports, because $U_\infty\Delta t = 0.25\,D$ sits *inside* the $0.5\,D$ tile overlap but *exactly on* the $0.25\,D$ band — the Schwarz-waveform-relaxation finite-step condition — while the one mechanism that would defeat one-pass exchange, elliptic pressure coupling, is **already global**. **Additive, never multiplicative**: sequential ordering reinstalls the graph-cycle path dependence W7 just exonerated *and* breaks [[symmetry-averaging-atlas-0.1]]'s exactness, since mirror-paired tiles would no longer see equivalent data — the first case of two composition-layer guarantees **conflicting**, and a gap in [[port-algebra-atlas-0.1]], which says nothing about port *resolution order*. Four stages, each able to end the sequence; **S0 instruments the interface residual against the existing loop and changes nothing**, and is the cheapest decisive measurement OP-5 has. S3 notes Atlas's 8 agents over 124 tiles is **already a two-level decomposition never used as one** — but a coarse solve is unavailable to the frozen expert, since coarsening means enlarging $L$ and $\mathrm{Re}_{\text{eff}}=T_s/(\nu_p L^2)$. **Built 2026-08-23, and the prediction became a proof:** the periodic sweep is a **constant map in the iteration index** — two passes agree *bitwise*, `np.array_equal` — so additive Schwarz on a periodic window is not weak, it is the **identity**; `schwarz > 1` is now refused at config time. `reference.WindowNS` supplies the missing boundary channel and is validated against exact answers first: uniform flow preserved **exactly** (the checkpoint gives 0.969), Taylor–Green second-order, projection divergence $7.6\times10^{-16}$, and a Gaussian wake diffusing within **0.44% of analytic** — the gate that matters, since a damping local solve would be worse than the defect. Two wrong turns kept: a projection that inverted the compact Laplacian while applying the wide one (interior divergence 2.5), and the MAC repair that would have been a $[1,2,1]/4$ filter per sub-step — $0.962^{51}\approx0.14$, an 86% loss at wake scales hidden inside a projection. The symmetry average had to learn to **mirror the transmission ring**, §4's predicted conflict arriving. Cost: a Dirichlet sweep is $\mathbf{14\times}$ the periodic one, all of it the Galilean frame — **a frozen expert cannot pay it at all**, which is the selection criterion rather than a limitation |
| ★★ **Open problems** *(deferred, with causes)* | [[open-problems-atlas-0.1]] | **Problems found by measurement, diagnosed to a cause, then deliberately deferred** — so picking one up later costs a read rather than a rediscovery. ## Key Cross-Cutting Themes

### "Train Once, Deploy Anywhere"
A single pretrained model that handles any physical system via in-context learning or few-shot finetuning. Tracked through [[poseidon-pde-foundation-model]] (629M params; operator learning; 15 OOD tasks from a 6-operator pretraining set), [[gphyt-physics-foundation-model]] (385M params; in-context), [[walrus-paper]] (1.3B params; autoregressive), [[aion-1-astronomy]] (39 modalities; masked). These four span the four training paradigms (operator / in-context-derivative / autoregressive / masked).

### Three PFM Training Paradigms (compared in [[pfm-architecture-approaches]])
- **Operator learning** — full trajectory from the initial datum: [[poseidon-pde-foundation-model]].
- **Autoregressive / in-context next-step** — [[walrus-paper]], [[gphyt-physics-foundation-model]].
- **Masked multimodal modeling** — [[aion-1-astronomy]].
Poseidon argues operator learning ≠ next-step prediction and shows it scales + generalizes.

### Unified Token Representation
The "no canonical data template" obstacle — how heterogeneous fields/grids/dimensions become tokens. Full per-representation treatment in [[00-token-representation-overview]]; broader taxonomy in [[multimodal-tokenization]].

### Physics Encoding Spectrum (weak → strong)
1. Data-driven only ([[walrus-paper]], [[poseidon-pde-foundation-model]], GP$_{\text{hy}}$T backbone)
2. Soft loss constraints ([[deeponet-multi-operator]])
3. Test-time physics guidance ([[pisd-physics-informed-spectral-diffusion]], [[noether-networks]])
4. Architectural soft biases ([[varmion-viscous-flows]], [[arch-gnn-physics-bottleneck]], [[multipole-graph-neural-operator]], [[anti-symmetric-dgn]])
5. Hard architectural constraints ([[pc-deeponet-cfd]], [[dynami-cal-graphnet]], [[hamiltonian-neural-networks]], [[lagrangian-neural-networks]])

*Full discussion: [[00-start-here]].*

### Generative vs. Deterministic Emulators
Strong evidence from [[latent-diffusion-physics]] that diffusion emulators outperform deterministic surrogates — critical for chaotic/turbulent systems.

### Polymathic AI Cluster
A coherent research program toward universal scientific foundation models: [[walrus-paper]], [[walrus-overview]], [[latent-diffusion-physics]], [[multiscale-diffusion-solar]], [[aion-1-astronomy]].
