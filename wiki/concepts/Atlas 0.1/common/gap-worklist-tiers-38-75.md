### The probed $S$ is the missing coarse space, and that is now measured rather than argued

[[atlas-and-standard-dd-theory]] §2.2 argues a geometric coarse space is unavailable to a frozen expert — enlarging the subdomain changes $\mathrm{Re}_{\text{eff}}$ rather than the resolution — and GenEO is unavailable because a checkpoint exposes no local eigenproblem; §3 names the **probed Schur complement** as the substitute. Priced in expert calls **including its own 34-call probe**, on the seam where the one-level method is slowest:

|---|---|---|---|
| Richardson, $\theta = 1/\lVert S\rVert$ | 192 | 288 | 594 |
| Richardson, $\alpha^\star$ undamped | *diverges* | *diverges* | *diverges* |
| Richardson, $\alpha^\star$ damped | 76 | 100 | 172 |
| Newton on the probed $S$ | **40** | **40** | **44** |

$4.8\times$ at the loose tolerance and $13.5\times$ at the tight one. In the *admitted* arrangement all four collapse to 38–52 calls because $\kappa$ is $1.2045$ and there is nothing left to accelerate — which corroborates §8.3's calibration pair ($21.73$ embedded, $1.196$ exposed) on a tiling and a state neither was taken at.

### What was fixed while building it

An artificial face's ring can **end on the domain boundary**: `C00`'s `yhi` ring spans the window's full width and its first cell sits at global $x = 0$, the inlet. Writing the iterate there let the domain edge drift — wrecking the edge by $7.0$ moved the sweep's output by $9.2\times10^{-6}$. `SchwarzExchange._domain_edge_mask` holds those cells, on the principle that **a cell on the domain boundary is a boundary cell whichever face of whichever window it belongs to**. The test that found it is kept, with a control showing an interior seam *does* move the sweep.

### And a note on what this does not reopen

**Nothing here rehabilitates fine-tuning.** The refusal that blocks substitution is R10 and R10 is about the receptive field, not the loss. This study's negative is about the **ceiling on a starting guess**, which no amount of training raises above one sweep. The two are independent and neither helps the other.

### W171 is scoped, and its [AI Inference] is FALSE

**The row's own instruction was to verify it rather than trust it, and the verification changes the shape of the work.** Three findings, all from `scripts/w171_decomposition_scope.py` and pinned in `tests/test_tier40_epsilon_halo.py`:

1. **The count is wrong in both numbers.** The row says *five rules branch on the axis and return early on the wrong one*. **Seven** functions read the axis, and **three** return having emitted nothing — `_halo_rule`, `_r12_conservative_assembly`, `_w49_sigma_branch`. The other four emit on both branches, so on the wrong axis they would say the wrong thing **loudly**, which is the cheaper failure and not the silent one the row is about. `_cut_policy` is the one that looks silent and is not: its wrong-axis branch emits `L2/C3` and *then* returns.
2. **The natural carrier is not the AGENT.** A cut is a **relation among several agents**, not a property of one: two agents of one family cut two different ways is one fact about one region and is unrepresentable on either agent. And an agent that is *not* cut — `front_wing`'s STRUCT, the sole elasticity agent — has no axis at all, so an agent-carried field needs a third state that `_decomposition_cuts` already computes separately.
