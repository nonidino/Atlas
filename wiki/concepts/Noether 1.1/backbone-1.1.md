# Backbone (Noether 1.1)

**Type:** Concept — model portion (folder: Noether 1.1)
**Status:** Design. The dynamics core — symmetric + skew-symmetric attention as a port-Hamiltonian integrator — carried forward from [[noether-1.0]] §3.5 / [[noether-1.0-rbc]] §4.5 with two 1.1 changes: it runs on the **typed multigraph** of field-token streams ([[field-token-streams-1.1]], [[edge-generation-1.1]]), and **momentum-antisymmetry is a gateable channel, not a mandatory structural law.**

## What it does — intuitively

Advance the tokens in latent time. Each layer is one small, stability-controlled Euler step of a learned flow that has two parts, matching the two things physics does: a **conservative** part that rotates latent state without gaining or losing "energy" (skew-symmetric), and a **dissipative** part that can bleed it (a learnable damping). Messages travel along the edges from [[edge-generation-1.1]], so the backbone learns *what force acts* along each edge type — the same weights inferring $1/r^2$ on orbits or stiff contact on grains, read from context.

The 1.1 change of emphasis: 1.0 made "interactions are equal-and-opposite" (Newton's third law) a **hard structural fact** of every message. That is right for transport and wrong for forcing (buoyancy, external drive, driven-dissipative systems). So 1.1 keeps the antisymmetric-flux machinery but puts it on a **gate**: default-on for same-field transport edges, learnably attenuated for cross-field / learned edges where the physics is genuinely not conservative.

## What it does — mathematically

Per layer $\ell$, over the typed multigraph, with ReZero gains $\alpha_\ell,\alpha'_\ell$ — one forward-Euler step of a learned flow ($h^{\ell+1}=h^\ell+\text{step}\times\tanh[\dots]$, so **depth is latent time**; stacking $L$ layers integrates an ODE $L$ times):
$$h_i^{\ell+1} = h_i^{\ell} + \alpha_\ell\,\epsilon\,\tanh\!\Big[\underbrace{(W_{\text{skew}}-W_{\text{skew}}^\top-\gamma_\ell I)h_i^\ell}_{\text{conservative rotation} + \text{learnable dissipation}} + \sum_{\text{types }\tau}\ \sum_{j\in\mathcal N_\tau(i)} g_\tau\, W_O^{(\tau)} A_{ij}^{(\tau)}\big(v_j^{(\tau)} - g_\tau v_i^{(\tau)}\big)\Big],$$
$$h_i^{\ell+1}\mathrel{+{=}}\alpha'_\ell\,\epsilon\,W_2\,\mathrm{renorm}\big(\mathrm{GELU}(W_1 h_i^{\ell+1})\big)\qquad\text{(F1 semi-orthogonal FFN sub-step — a second, separately-gained mini-integrator, run per token).}$$

**Where $v^{(\tau)}$ and $A_{ij}^{(\tau)}$ come from — the per-type attention, expanded.** Each edge type $\tau$ runs $H$ heads of width $d_h=d/H$ with **tied $Q{=}K$** (the same projection plays both roles):
$$q_i^{(\tau,k)}=k_i^{(\tau,k)}=W^{(\tau,k)}h_i,\qquad v_i^{(\tau,k)}=W_V^{(\tau,k)}h_i,\qquad A_{ij}^{(\tau,k)}=\tanh\!\Big(\tfrac{q_i^{(\tau,k)}\cdot q_j^{(\tau,k)}}{\sqrt{d_h}}\Big),$$
and the per-type message above is the sum over heads $k$ of these (each with its own $v^{(\tau,k)}$), combined after the up-projection $W_O^{(\tau)}$. Two consequences: **tying $Q{=}K$ makes the score reciprocal by construction** ($q_i\cdot q_j=q_j\cdot q_i$) — $i$'s pull toward $j$ equals $j$'s pull toward $i$, the "equal strength" half of Newton's third law; the antisymmetric flux $(v_j-g_\tau v_i)$ below supplies the "opposite direction" half — together, at $g_\tau=1$, both halves hold and the interaction is exactly Newton's third law, split into two independently-enforced mechanisms. **Heads are a basis of coupling operators** ([[multihead-attention]]): each head's $W^{(\tau,k)},W_V^{(\tau,k)}$ can specialize (attractive at range, repulsive at contact, …), and summing — not concatenating — the up-projected heads combines them into the one real force acting on that edge type. **On summing rather than averaging heads — is there a normalization issue?** Summing $H$ heads scales the pre-$\tanh$ message by $\sim\!\sqrt H$ (independent heads) up to $\sim\!H$ (aligned), but this is *not* a norm-drift problem and must **not** be "fixed" with a $1/H$ average: the heads are *additive* coupling operators (buoyancy $+$ pressure $+$ viscous$\dots$), so their sum is the net force and averaging would systematically shrink it as operators are added — the opposite of language-MHA heads, which are interchangeable subspaces. The steady-state scale is absorbed by the learned ReZero gain $\alpha_\ell$ (init $0$), so no LayerNorm is needed to tame it ([[normalization-scheme]]). The one real concern is **$\tanh$ saturation at initialization** if many heads sum before the nonlinearity; the clean fix is a *constant* $1/\sqrt H$ scale folded into the message (equivalently, ReZero's small-$\alpha$ init), which keeps the block in $\tanh$'s linear regime early without touching the additive-force semantics — and, being constant, preserves the $g_\tau{=}1$ telescoping exactly (only a *learned per-head* mix would reintroduce the $W_O$ asymmetry [[multihead-attention]] warns against).

Key pieces:

- **Per-edge-type messages.** Each edge type $\tau$ (same-field, cross-field co-located, cross-field spatial, learned) has its own $A^{(\tau)}, W_O^{(\tau)}$ — "velocity→temperature" is a different operator from "velocity→velocity" ([[field-token-streams-1.1]]).
- **Gated antisymmetry.** $g_\tau\in[0,1]$ is a learnable per-type gate on the flux symmetry. At $g_\tau=1$ the aggregation is the antisymmetric $\sum_j A_{ij}(v_j-v_i)$ with $\sum_i\Delta_i=0$ (exact momentum conservation, as in 1.0) — whatever $i$ gains, $j$ loses, exactly, so it telescopes to zero over the whole graph. At $g_\tau<1$ the residual $\big(1-g_\tau\big)\sum_j A_{ij}v_i$ no longer cancels — the message degenerates toward a one-way source with no reaction subtracted out, the mechanism for non-conservative forcing (buoyancy, drive). Default init $g=1$ on same-field edges, $g$ free on the rest.
- **Parameterize the gate so it can reach the endpoints *exactly*.** A sigmoid is the wrong primitive here: it approaches $1$ only as its pre-activation $\to+\infty$, so it parks near $0.99$ and the un-cancelled residual leaks a little momentum *every layer*, accumulating roughly **linearly** over a rollout — at $g=0.99$, $10^3$ steps carry $\sim\!10\times$ the per-step leak. Use instead a **clamped/hardtanh gate** $g_\tau=\operatorname{clamp}(a_\tau,0,1)$ (or the one-sided $g_\tau=1-\operatorname{ReLU}(a_\tau)$), which hits **exactly $1$ on the open set $a_\tau\ge1$** — and exactly $0$ for $a_\tau\le0$, the pure-forcing endpoint — is linear in between, and has zero gradient at the rails so a genuinely-conservative edge *locks in* and stays. The prediction loss still supplies gradient through the interior to open the gate when forcing lowers error, so "sticky at $1$" is a feature, not a trap. A hard-concrete / straight-through gate (the relaxation family [[edge-generation-1.1]] already uses) is the alternative when a live escape gradient *at* the rail is wanted.
- **What the exact gate does and does not buy — the soft/hard split.** Even at $g_\tau=1$ *exactly*, the layer is **not** exactly momentum-conserving, because the per-node $\tanh$ (and the $\alpha_\ell\epsilon$ gain) wrap the aggregated message: $\sum_i\tanh(\Delta_i)\ne\tanh(\sum_i\Delta_i)$. The telescoping is a property of the *pre-nonlinearity* aggregate — of **depth**, not of the decoded physical trajectory — exactly [[open-architectural-problems]] Problem 1 (depth-conservation $\ne$ time-conservation). So the exact-$1$ gate sharpens the *inductive-bias / soft* conservation and shrinks what the corrector must clean up; the **hard** guarantee against rollout drift is the live projection in [[discovered-conservation-1.1]], re-run every step and gated on measured held-out drift. Read together: a slightly leaky gate on a *hard-gated* quantity is harmless (the projection catches it); the leak matters only for quantities left on the soft channel, where the honest position is to *measure and report* the residual drift, not to claim zero.
- **Cayley-orthogonal $W_{\text{skew}}$** (P10.1): parameterized as $W_{\text{skew}}=(I-S)(I+S)^{-1}$ of a learned skew generator $S$, which is *orthogonal* by construction — this analytically bounds $\|W_{\text{skew}}-W_{\text{skew}}^\top\|_2\le2$ (not just at init, *through training*), so the Euler-stability condition $\epsilon<2/\|\cdot\|_2$ holds without having to police it empirically.
- **$\gamma_\ell$ learnable, per-layer, sign-unconstrained** — the conservative/dissipative dial the model sets from data (the same instinct [[discovered-conservation-1.1]] generalizes to explicit invariants).
- **No LayerNorm** ([[normalization-scheme]]); ReZero gains give depth control instead — every operation in the block (skew rotation, antisymmetric flux, semi-orthogonal FFN) is chosen to be close to norm-preserving by construction, so the small amount of norm change that does happen is exactly what $\gamma_\ell$ and $g_\tau$ control on purpose, not incidental drift.

## Physics relevance

- **Port-Hamiltonian structure.** Conservative (skew) + dissipative ($-\gamma I$) + external ports (gated non-conservative edges) is exactly the port-Hamiltonian decomposition of an open physical system — the honest generalization of a Hamiltonian core to *driven-dissipative* physics, which is most real physics ([[hamiltonian-message-passing]], [[skew-symmetric-attention]]).
- **Momentum without mandating it.** The gate resolves the tension the RBC build exposed: momentum conservation should be *available and cheap* (particles, inviscid transport) without being *forced* where gravity or drive breaks it. The structural law becomes a strong prior the data can switch off.
- **Attention = learnable graph Laplacian.** Symmetric scores on the radius graph are a learned diffusion/coupling operator; the hierarchy edges carry the elliptic far-field — the same reading as [[symmetric-attention-physics]], now over typed edges.

## How fundamental constants are included

- **Constants enter as conditioning bias on every token, not as backbone weights.** $z_{\text{cond}}$ ([[conditioning-and-constants-1.1]]) is added to each $h_i$, so the same backbone runs different regimes; no dimensionless group is baked into $W_{\text{skew}}$, $A^{(\tau)}$, or $\gamma_\ell$.
- **Vector constants act through the covariant channel** carried on the relevant field stream (gravity on velocity, not temperature), so the backbone can produce an anisotropic, buoyancy-like message *only* when the arrow is present. There are two concrete, equivariance-safe entry points into the layer: **(i)** as **rotation-invariant contractions** that modulate the score/gate — $\langle\gamma_{\text{vec}},x_j-x_i\rangle$ or $\|\gamma_{\text{vec}}\|$ feeding $A^{(\tau)}_{ij}$ or $g_\tau$, which is *how gravity makes vertical edges behave differently from horizontal ones*; and **(ii)** as an **additive vector source term** on the vector-valued value channel (a body force added into the velocity increment). Entry (ii) is exactly a non-conservative forcing, so it is the *same site* where $g_\tau$ must be free to drop below $1$ — the arrow and the open antisymmetry gate coincide. In the **default setting (no hard equivariance, [[open-architectural-problems]] Problem 9)** the vector simply enters as ordinary component channels on the stream's tokens, processed like $z_{\text{cond}}$, with equivariance learned by augmentation; the invariant-contraction / F2-gated-nonlinearity treatment above is the *hard-equivariant upgrade*. Either way no vector constant is a backbone weight — it rides the token and rotates correctly with the frame.
- **The dissipation dial $\gamma_\ell$ is learned, not set from a constant** — even viscosity enters as a conditioning group, letting the model discover the effective dissipation rather than reading it off a hard-coded $\nu$.
