## Overview

Dissipative Hamiltonian Neural Networks (D-HNNs) extend HNNs to handle systems with energy dissipation. The architectural move: parameterize **two** scalar networks — a Hamiltonian $H_\theta(q, p)$ and a Rayleigh dissipation function $D_\phi(q, \dot{q})$. Together they realize the **Helmholtz decomposition** of the dynamics into a divergence-free (Hamiltonian, conservative) component and a gradient-like (dissipative) component. The decomposition is interpretable: $H_\theta$ recovers the conserved energy in the limit of no dissipation; $D_\phi$ recovers the friction/damping function.

D-HNN is the cleanest existing demonstration in the neural-physics literature of **separately learnable conservative and dissipative dynamics** — a critical capability for any model intended to handle the full spectrum of real physics, which is almost always at least mildly dissipative.

---

## Background: Helmholtz Decomposition

Any smooth vector field $f(x)$ on phase space $\mathbb{R}^{2d}$ admits (under mild conditions) a decomposition

$$f(x) = f_{\text{cons}}(x) + f_{\text{diss}}(x)$$

into a divergence-free part $f_{\text{cons}}$ (Hamiltonian-like, preserves volume) and a gradient-like part $f_{\text{diss}}$ (dissipative). The conservative part is generated by a Hamiltonian via $f_{\text{cons}} = J \nabla H$ where

$$J = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & I_d \\ -I_d & 0 \end{pmatrix}$$

is the symplectic matrix. The dissipative part, for a mechanical system, is generated by a **Rayleigh dissipation function** $D: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ — a non-negative scalar function of the velocity.

The Lagrange-d'Alembert principle with Rayleigh dissipation gives:

$$\dot{q} = \frac{\partial H}{\partial p}, \qquad \dot{p} = -\frac{\partial H}{\partial q} - \frac{\partial D}{\partial \dot{q}}$$

The dissipated power is $2D$; total energy $H$ decreases at rate $-2D \leq 0$, with equality at rest.

## D-HNN Method

### Architecture

Two neural networks:

- $H_\theta(q, p) \in \mathbb{R}$ — scalar Hamiltonian
- $D_\phi(q, \dot{q}) \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ — scalar Rayleigh dissipation

The predicted dynamics:

$$\hat{\dot{q}} = \frac{\partial H_\theta}{\partial p}, \qquad \hat{\dot{p}} = -\frac{\partial H_\theta}{\partial q} - \frac{\partial D_\phi}{\partial \dot{q}}$$

### Loss

Standard L2 on time derivatives, identical to HNN:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{D-HNN}}(\theta, \phi) = \|\dot{q} - \hat{\dot{q}}\|^2 + \|\dot{p} - \hat{\dot{p}}\|^2$$

The architectural split forces the model to route non-conservative dynamics into $D_\phi$ rather than corrupting $H_\theta$.

### Non-Negativity of $D$

For $D_\phi$ to act as a true Rayleigh dissipation function, it should be non-negative. This is enforced softly (output through a non-negative activation like softplus or squared output) rather than as a hard constraint — empirically sufficient.

### Reduction to HNN

When the system is genuinely conservative, training drives $D_\phi \to 0$, recovering HNN as a special case. D-HNN is strictly more expressive than HNN.

## Empirical Results

The paper demonstrates D-HNN on:

1. **Damped mass-spring** (linear dissipation): D-HNN learns $H_\theta = \frac{1}{2}(p^2/m + kq^2)$ as the harmonic Hamiltonian and $D_\phi = \frac{1}{2}c\dot{q}^2$ as a quadratic Rayleigh function with $c$ matching the friction coefficient.

2. **Damped pendulum:** Similar success; the model cleanly disentangles the conservative pendulum dynamics from the velocity-proportional drag.

3. **Prediction across friction coefficients:** Once trained on one damped system, the model can predict dynamics for *unseen* friction coefficients by simply rescaling the learned $D_\phi$. This works because the structure of $D_\phi$ has been learned correctly; only the magnitude needs adjustment. This is significantly more parameter-efficient than retraining for each friction level.

## Properties

1. **Interpretable decomposition.** $H_\theta$ and $D_\phi$ can be inspected separately. For systems with known conservative structure (energy = T + V), $H_\theta$ should match the analytical form up to additive constant.

2. **Energy budget tracking.** $dH_\theta/dt = -2D_\phi$ gives a direct learned account of where energy goes. For a real dissipative system, this can be checked against measured power dissipation.

3. **Generalizes across dissipation regimes.** Different friction levels, fluid viscosities, or damping conditions can be handled by rescaling $D_\phi$ without retraining $H_\theta$.

4. **Compatible with HGN-style graph architectures.** A graph D-HNN parameterizes per-system $H$ and $D$ as sums over local graph contributions — making the framework applicable to many-body simulations.

---

## Limitations

1. **Limited to Helmholtz-decomposable dissipation.** Coulomb friction (non-smooth at zero velocity), hysteretic damping (memory-dependent), and turbulent eddy dissipation (nonlocal in scale) all violate the assumptions. For these, $D_\phi$ either fails to fit or fits with degraded structure.

2. **Decomposition not unique.** The Helmholtz decomposition is unique only up to gauge — adding $\nabla \times A$ to $f_{\text{cons}}$ and subtracting from $f_{\text{diss}}$ is invisible. Empirically the optimization tends to favor minimal $D_\phi$, but no theoretical guarantee.

3. **Canonical coordinates still required.** Like HNN, D-HNN needs $(q, p)$ form. The Lagrangian dissipative variant (Lagrange-d'Alembert with Rayleigh) avoids this — see [[lagrangian-neural-networks]].

4. **Local Rayleigh function.** $D_\phi$ depends on $(q, \dot{q})$ pointwise. Spatially nonlocal dissipation (turbulence) requires extending $D_\phi$ to depend on neighborhoods — typically through a GNN over the system.

## Relevance to Physics Foundation Models

The Hamiltonian + dissipative split is one of the most physically principled architectural decompositions for any model intended to span real-world physics:

1. **Real physics is almost always dissipative.** Pure HNN is too restrictive; D-HNN is the realistic minimum.

2. **The split improves data efficiency.** Once the conservative structure is learned, dissipation parameters can be fit per-task with very few additional samples.

3. **Compatible with conservation guarantees.** A graph-based D-HNN with Dynami-CAL antisymmetric edge frames simultaneously has:
   - Exact linear + angular momentum conservation (Dynami-CAL)
   - Exact energy conservation in the conservative limit (H-part)
   - Learned dissipative correction (D-part)
   
   This is the level-5 (hard constraint) physics encoding across all three primary conservation laws — currently the strongest known design point.

4. **Interpretable for scientific discovery.** A trained D-HNN can be queried for the learned Hamiltonian and dissipation function separately, enabling sparse-regression / symbolic distillation to recover analytic forms.

## Connection to Port-Hamiltonian Systems

Port-Hamiltonian systems (PHS) generalize Hamiltonian mechanics to **open** systems with external ports through which energy flows in/out:

$$\dot{x} = [J(x) - R(x)] \nabla H(x) + g(x) u, \qquad y = g(x)^\top \nabla H(x)$$

where $J$ is skew-symmetric (Hamiltonian part), $R$ is symmetric positive-semidefinite (dissipation), and $(u, y)$ is the input/output port pair. Port-Hamiltonian neural networks (Massaroli et al. 2019; Furieri et al. 2022) extend D-HNN to learn $J, R, H$ jointly. D-HNN is the closed-port special case.

For a PFM, port-Hamiltonian extensions are necessary to handle:
- External time-varying forcing (driven oscillators, boundary conditions)
- Coupled multi-physics (energy flows between subsystems)
- Control inputs (robotic manipulation, plasma control)
