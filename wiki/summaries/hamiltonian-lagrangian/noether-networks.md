## Overview

Noether Networks introduce a meta-learning framework that **discovers** conserved quantities rather than hardcoding them. The architecture combines:

1. A standard prediction model $f_\theta$ (e.g., next-frame predictor, dynamics model).
2. An auxiliary network $g_\phi$ that outputs a putative conserved quantity.
3. A **test-time tailoring** procedure that adjusts $\theta$ on each test trajectory to enforce conservation of $g_\phi$.
4. A meta-learning outer loop that trains $\phi$ so that this tailoring is maximally useful for prediction.

This inverts the standard approach to symmetry in ML: rather than enumerate known symmetries and bake them in (CNN translation equivariance, SE(3)-equivariant networks), Noether Networks learn which conservation law is useful for the task and enforce it via meta-learning. Inspired by **Noether's theorem** — for every continuous symmetry of the action, there is a corresponding conserved quantity.

---

## Background: Noether's Theorem

Emmy Noether's 1918 theorem is the deepest statement linking symmetry and conservation in physics:

**Theorem (Noether, informal):** Every continuous one-parameter symmetry of the action functional $S[q]$ corresponds to a conserved quantity along the equations of motion.

| Symmetry of the action | Conserved quantity |
|---|---|
| Time translation $t \to t + \epsilon$ | Energy $E$ |
| Spatial translation $q \to q + \epsilon$ | Linear momentum $p$ |
| Spatial rotation $q \to R q$ | Angular momentum $L$ |
| Galilean boost | Center of mass motion |
| Global phase $\psi \to e^{i\theta}\psi$ | Electric charge / particle number |
| Scale (in scale-invariant theories) | Virial / dilatation current |
| Local gauge $\psi \to e^{i\theta(x)}\psi$ | Gauge current (e.g., electromagnetic) |

For a PFM, the practical implication is that *every relevant physical regime has its own set of conserved quantities*, and the set is rich (energy, momentum, angular momentum, charge, particle number, vorticity, helicity, ...). Hardcoding all of them is impractical; discovering them is the right approach at scale.

## Method

### Three Networks

- $f_\theta$: prediction model. Maps $x_t$ to $\hat{x}_{t+1}$ (or rolls out a trajectory).
- $g_\phi$: conserved-quantity network. Maps state $x$ to a $k$-dimensional vector that "should be" conserved.
- An adaptation procedure parameterized by step size $\eta$ and number of inner steps.

### Conservation Loss

For a predicted trajectory $\hat{x}_1, \ldots, \hat{x}_T$:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}}(\theta, \phi; x_{1:T}) = \sum_{t=1}^{T-1} \| g_\phi(\hat{x}_{t+1}) - g_\phi(\hat{x}_t) \|^2$$

If $g_\phi$ is perfectly conserved along the predicted trajectory, this is zero. Drift produces a positive loss.

### Inner Loop: Test-Time Tailoring

At inference, given a new test trajectory beginning $x_1$, the model rolls out a prediction $\hat{x}_{1:T}$ and adapts $\theta$ to minimize $\mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}}$:

$$\theta_{\text{tailored}}(x_1, \phi) = \theta - \eta \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}}(\theta, \phi; \hat{x}_{1:T})$$

This is MAML-style fast adaptation, but the adaptation signal comes from **self-consistency with $g_\phi$**, not from test labels. No test-time supervision is required.

### Outer Loop: Meta-Learning

The outer loop trains $\phi$ so that the tailored prediction $f_{\theta_{\text{tailored}}}$ is good on the prediction loss:

$$\phi^* = \arg\min_\phi \mathbb{E}_{x_{1:T}\sim \mathcal{D}}\left[\mathcal{L}_{\text{pred}}\!\left(f_{\theta_{\text{tailored}}(x_1, \phi)}(x_1), x_{1:T}\right)\right]$$

Meta-gradients flow through the inner-loop update via implicit differentiation or unrolled backprop (truncated for tractability).

### Key Conceptual Move

$g_\phi$ is **not constrained to be a true physical conserved quantity**. The only constraint is that *enforcing its conservation at test time improves prediction*. The meta-learning objective discovers $g_\phi$ that satisfies this property. In effect, the model learns "what is approximately conserved in this dataset, and would be useful to enforce at inference."

## Theoretical Properties

The paper establishes (under mild conditions):

1. **Recovery of true conservation laws.** If the data is generated by a process with a true conserved quantity $g^*$, then meta-learning $g_\phi$ converges to a function in the same equivalence class as $g^*$ (up to invertible transformations).
2. **Prediction error reduction.** Tailoring reduces test-time error when $g_\phi$ is closely related to a real conserved quantity.
3. **Consistency.** Asymptotically, $g_\phi$ converges to the most useful conservation structure for the task.

---

## Empirical Results

The paper demonstrates Noether Networks on:

1. **Physics systems with known conservation laws** (pendulum, mass-spring, ideal gas): the discovered $g_\phi$ correlates strongly with the true conserved quantity (energy or momentum, depending on the system).

2. **Moving MNIST video prediction**: improves prediction quality over baseline predictors, and over models with hardcoded translation equivariance — i.e., the discovered conservation is more useful than the hardcoded one.

3. **Real-world video**: continues to improve prediction quality, demonstrating that meta-learned conservation laws transfer to systems without simple known conservation.

4. **Ablations**: removing the meta-learning (using a fixed $g_\phi$) substantially degrades performance; removing the tailoring (using $g_\phi$ only as a training-time loss) does likewise. The full bilevel structure is needed.

---

## Strengths

1. **Domain-agnostic.** Applies wherever there's a sequence to predict; no knowledge of the underlying physics required.
2. **Discovers approximate conservation.** Real-world data often has *approximately* conserved quantities; Noether captures these without forcing exact conservation.
3. **Model-agnostic wrapper.** Works around any prediction architecture (CNN, RNN, transformer, GNN, diffusion).
4. **Combines naturally with hard-constraint approaches.** Hard architectural constraints (Dynami-CAL, HNN) cover known conservation laws; Noether discovers additional useful ones.

---

## Limitations

1. **Computational cost.** Bilevel optimization (inner tailoring + outer meta-training) is significantly more expensive than standard training. Implicit-differentiation tricks and truncated unrolling are necessary at scale.

2. **No exact conservation.** Unlike Dynami-CAL (exact momentum) or HNN (exact energy), the discovered $g_\phi$ is only approximately conserved. For chaotic systems with long rollouts, this may accumulate errors.

3. **Discovery, not creation.** If the dataset has no useful conserved structure, Noether has nothing to find.

4. **Tailoring at inference is non-trivial.** Test-time gradient updates require an optimizer step at inference; this adds latency and complicates deployment compared to a single-shot forward pass.

5. **Multiple conserved quantities.** If $g_\phi$ outputs a vector, the components might collapse to a single direction. Regularization or orthogonality constraints are needed for multi-quantity discovery.

## Relation to Other Conservation Approaches

| Approach | Conservation type | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Dynami-CAL | Exact (linear+angular momentum) | When the system has Newton's-3rd-law forces |
| HNN / LNN | Exact (energy in conservative limit) | When system is genuinely conservative |
| D-HNN | Exact decomposition (conservative + dissipative) | When you need to separate the two |
| PC-DeepONet | Exact (divergence-free, etc.) | When constraint is known and structural |
| Noether Networks | Approximate, discovered | When conservation law is unknown or system is partially dissipative |
| Soft loss penalties | Approximate, hand-specified | Quick baseline when you know the conservation law |

Noether Networks are uniquely positioned for **unknown-system regimes** where the relevant conserved quantity is not obvious a priori — a key part of the PFM use case.

---

## Relevance to Physics Foundation Models

A PFM intended to cover diverse regimes (fluid, granular, plasma, biological, structural) faces a combinatorial problem: each regime has its own conservation laws (NS conserves mass + momentum + energy; MHD adds magnetic helicity; ideal gas adds particle number; etc.). Hardcoding every conservation law for every regime is impractical.

Noether Networks offer a path:

1. **Pretrain on diverse regimes** with hard conservation for well-understood laws (momentum via Dynami-CAL, divergence-free via PC-DeepONet).
2. **Meta-learn $g_\phi$** during pretraining to discover regime-specific approximate conservation.
3. **At deployment**, tailor to the test regime using only its initial trajectory — recovering the relevant approximate conserved quantity automatically.

This is the cleanest existing framework for *transfer* of conservation structure across physical regimes — and the natural complement to Lagrangian Neural Networks, which provide the action-based backbone.

## [AI Inference]

**[AI Inference]:** Combining LNN + Noether Networks gives an architecture that hard-conserves quantities arising from explicit symmetries of $L_\theta$ (energy from time translation, momentum from spatial translation, ...) *and* soft-conserves additional quantities discovered by meta-learning. This is the most expressive symmetry/conservation framework currently available; no current PFM architecture in the wiki integrates both. A concept page formalizing this composition is in [[action-based-noether-enforcement]].

**[AI Inference]:** Noether Networks' tailoring step is structurally identical to **test-time training** (TTT) for vision transformers and to **in-context learning** in LLMs: all three modify model behavior at inference using unlabeled context. The connection to in-context learning is direct — for physics, the "in-context" signal is conservation of a discovered quantity, which is itself a fundamental form of self-consistency. This suggests that physics ICL, currently demonstrated in GP$_\text{hy}$T ([[gphyt-physics-foundation-model]]), can be substantially enhanced by adding Noether-style tailoring on top of the standard transformer in-context mechanism.

**[AI Inference]:** The discovered $g_\phi$ provides a natural **scientific interpretability** lens. By inspecting $g_\phi$ for trained Noether Networks on different physical regimes, researchers can extract what the model considers approximately conserved — and compare against known conservation laws. This gives a path to using neural physics models for *discovery*, not just prediction.
