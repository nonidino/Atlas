4.1 T5: Theorem 1 and Corollary 1

In plain words.

If the iterates settle anywhere, they settle on a fixed point of the classical map. Nothing about the accuracy of the cheap map enters, only that both maps are continuous at the limit. A wrong cheap map can cost classical calls; it cannot change what is returned. And if the cheap map is constant, the iteration is the classical march itself.

Let \(E\) be a normed space and \(\Phi ,\Psi :E\to E\). The point \(w'\) is a successor of \(w\) if \(w'-\Psi (w')=(w-\Psi (w))-(w-\Phi (w))\). The inner march that solves for it is \(x^{(0)}=\Phi (w)\), \(x^{(m+1)}=\Phi (w)+[\Psi (x^{(m)})-\Psi (w)]\).

Let \(w_{k+1}\) be a successor of \(w_k\) for every \(k\), and \(w_k\to \bar w\). If \(\Phi \) and \(\Psi \) are continuous at \(\bar w\), then \(\Phi (\bar w)=\bar w\).

Proof ▶

\(w_k-\Phi (w_k)=\bigl(w_k-\Psi (w_k)\bigr)-\bigl(w_{k+1}-\Psi (w_{k+1})\bigr)\). The right side tends to \((\bar w-\Psi (\bar w))-(\bar w-\Psi (\bar w))=0\) and the left to \(\bar w-\Phi (\bar w)\).
