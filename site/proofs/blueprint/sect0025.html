7.1 T8: wave variables and the Cayley transform

In plain words.

On a side, the effort \(e\) (a temperature) and the flow \(f\) (a flux into the piece) are combined into an incoming wave \(e+Zf\) and an outgoing wave \(e-Zf\), for an impedance \(Z{\gt}0\). The squared size of the outgoing wave is that of the incoming one minus four times \(Z\) times the power the piece absorbs. So a piece that never generates power does not amplify waves, and a piece that absorbs a definite share of what it is given shrinks them by a definite factor.

In a real inner-product space, for all \(e,f\) and all real \(Z\),

\[ \lVert e-Zf\rVert ^2=\lVert e+Zf\rVert ^2-4Z\, \langle f,e\rangle . \]

Let \(Z{\gt}0\), \(c\ge 0\), and let \(e,f\) satisfy \(\langle f,e\rangle \ge c\lVert e\rVert ^2\) and \(\lVert f\rVert \le M\lVert e\rVert \). Then

\[ \lVert e-Zf\rVert ^2\le \Bigl(1-\frac{4Zc}{(1+ZM)^2}\Bigr)\lVert e+Zf\rVert ^2 . \]

In particular, for a bounded linear \(\Lambda \) with \(\langle \Lambda e,e\rangle \ge c\lVert e\rVert ^2\) for all \(e\) and \(\lVert \Lambda \rVert \le M\), this holds with \(f=\Lambda e\) for every \(e\).

Proof ▶

With \(y=e+Zf\): \(\lVert e-Zf\rVert ^2=\lVert y\rVert ^2-4Z\langle f,e\rangle \le \lVert y\rVert ^2-4Zc\lVert e\rVert ^2\), and \(\lVert y\rVert \le (1+ZM)\lVert e\rVert \).

Let \(E\) be a finite-dimensional real inner-product space, not the zero space, and \(\Lambda \) as in Theorem 79. Then \(I+Z\Lambda \) is invertible and \(\mathcal S=(I-Z\Lambda )(I+Z\Lambda )^{-1}\) satisfies

\[ \lVert \mathcal S\rVert ^2\le 1-\frac{4Zc}{(1+ZM)^2}. \]

If only \(\langle \Lambda e,e\rangle \ge 0\) for all \(e\), then \(I+Z\Lambda \) is invertible and \(\lVert \mathcal S\rVert \le 1\) (on any finite-dimensional space).

Proof ▶

\(\langle (I+Z\Lambda )e,e\rangle \ge \lVert e\rVert ^2\), so \(I+Z\Lambda \) is injective, hence invertible in finite dimensions. Every \(y\) is \((I+Z\Lambda )e\) for one \(e\), and \(\mathcal Sy=(I-Z\Lambda )e\); Theorem 79.
