1.2 T7: the a-posteriori certificate

In plain words.

A solver hands back a state \(w\). Its residual, how far one more classical step moves it, can be computed. Its error, how far it is from the settled state \(w^\star \), cannot, because \(w^\star \) is unknown. The certificate bounds the second by the first: if the step brings \(w\) closer to \(w^\star \) by a factor \(L{\lt}1\), the error is at most the residual divided by \(1-L\).

What the constant must be.

The factor \(L\) must hold at the state being certified. A constant read off one approach to \(w^\star \) says nothing about a state that approaches from another direction. For a linear step this is exact: the smallest constant that certifies every state is an operator norm, a supremum over all directions (Theorem 6). The project met this the hard way: a constant read off one march under-bounded two of seven solver arms on its largest test.

Theorem 4 T7, the certificate

Let \((X,d)\) be a metric space, \(\Phi :X\to X\), and \(w,w^\star \in X\) with \(\Phi (w^\star )=w^\star \). If \(d(\Phi w,\Phi w^\star )\le L\, d(w,w^\star )\) with \(L{\lt}1\), then

\[ d(w,w^\star )\le \frac{d(w,\Phi w)}{1-L}. \]
Proof ▶

\(d(w,w^\star )\le d(w,\Phi w)+d(\Phi w,\Phi w^\star )\le d(w,\Phi w)+L\, d(w,w^\star )\), and \(1-L{\gt}0\).

Let \(X\) be a non-empty complete metric space and \(d(\Phi x,\Phi y)\le L\, d(x,y)\) for all \(x,y\), with \(0\le L{\lt}1\). Then \(\Phi \) has a unique fixed point \(w^\star \), and \(d(w,w^\star )\le d(w,\Phi w)/(1-L)\) for every \(w\in X\).

Proof ▶

Banach’s theorem gives \(w^\star \); Theorem 4 applies to every \(w\). (Mathlib: ContractingWith.dist_fixedPoint_le.)

Theorem 6 T7 for a linear step: the constant is an operator norm
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • Atlas.certificate_constant_is_opNorm

Let \(E\) be a real normed space, \(A:E\to E\) bounded and linear, \(b\in E\), and \(\Phi (w)=Aw+b\). Suppose \(I-A\) has a bounded inverse \(B\), and \(\Phi (w^\star )=w^\star \). Then

  1. \(\lVert w-w^\star \rVert \le \lVert B\rVert \, \lVert \Phi (w)-w\rVert \) for every \(w\in E\);

  2. if \(C\ge 0\) and \(\lVert w-w^\star \rVert \le C\, \lVert \Phi (w)-w\rVert \) for every \(w\in E\), then \(\lVert B\rVert \le C\).

So \(\lVert (I-A)^{-1}\rVert \) is the least constant that certifies every state.

Proof ▶

\(\Phi (w)-w=(A-I)(w-w^\star )\), so \(w-w^\star =-B(\Phi (w)-w)\), which gives 1. For 2, given \(x\in E\) let \(w=w^\star +Bx\). Then \(\Phi (w)-w=(A-I)Bx=-x\), and the hypothesis reads \(\lVert Bx\rVert \le C\lVert x\rVert \). This holds for every \(x\), so \(\lVert B\rVert \le C\).

Example 7 The two-mode example
✓

For \(\Phi (x,y)=(0.6\, x,\ 0.95\, y)\) on \(\mathbb {R}^2\) with the maximum norm, whose settled state is \(0\): the error of \((1,0)\) is \(2.5\) times its residual, and the error of \((0,1)\) is \(20\) times its residual. A constant of \(2.5\), read off a march that stays in the fast mode, under-bounds the slow mode by a factor of eight.

Proof ▶

\(\Phi (1,0)-(1,0)=(-0.4,\, 0)\) and \(\Phi (0,1)-(0,1)=(0,\, -0.05)\), whose maximum norms are \(0.4\) and \(0.05\), while \(\lVert (1,0)\rVert =\lVert (0,1)\rVert =1\); and \(2.5\cdot 0.4=20\cdot 0.05=1\).
