8.3 T2: existence, as a conditional theorem

In plain words.

Can a learned coupling do as well as the classical one? If a network can approximate the classical interface map and its derivative to within \(\eta \) on a ball around the classical answer, then its iteration contracts at the classical rate plus \(\eta \) and its answer is within \(\eta /(1-\rho -\eta )\) of the classical one. The “if” is the universal approximation theorem in \(C^1\), which is cited and not proved here. Whether a trained network meets it is measured, one network at a time.

Let \(E\) be a real Banach space, \(B\) the closed ball of radius \(r\ge 0\) around \(a_c\), \(T_c(a_c)=a_c\) and \(\lVert T_cx-T_cy\rVert \le \rho \lVert x-y\rVert \) on \(B\). Let \(T_l\) satisfy \(\lVert T_la_c-T_ca_c\rVert \le \eta \) and \(\lVert (T_l-T_c)x-(T_l-T_c)y\rVert \le \eta \lVert x-y\rVert \) on \(B\) (which holds if \(T_l-T_c\) has a derivative of norm at most \(\eta \) on \(B\)). If \(\rho +\eta {\lt}1\) and \(\eta \le (1-\rho -\eta )\, r\), then \(T_l\) has exactly one fixed point \(a_l\) in \(B\); from every \(x_0\in B\) the iterates stay in \(B\) and \(\lVert T_l^{\, k}x_0-a_l\rVert \le (\rho +\eta )^k\lVert x_0-a_l\rVert \); and

\[ \lVert a_l-a_c\rVert \le \frac{\eta }{1-\rho -\eta }. \]

Consequently, if a class of maps contains one such \(T_l\), it contains a map with these three properties.

Proof ▶

\(T_l=T_c+(T_l-T_c)\) is \((\rho +\eta )\)-Lipschitz on \(B\), and \(\lVert T_lx-a_c\rVert \le (\rho +\eta )r+\eta \le r\), so \(T_l\) maps \(B\) into itself. \(B\) is complete; Theorem 1 on \(B\). The derivative form is the mean value inequality on the convex set \(B\).
