The question

A certificate you cannot fail is not a certificate

Wake steering and micro-siting move a wind farm's annual energy yield by several percent, so the accuracy of the wake model is worth real money. That makes it a good place to ask a harder question than “is the model accurate?” — namely, whether a composed simulation built on a pretrained expert can detect and attribute that expert's error without anyone having to know the answer in advance.

This is the sixth case study in the vault whose experts are real rather than hand-written, and the first whose geometry is real. Its predecessor has the wind farm's topology with real experts and says of itself that its geometry is schematic — six windows of one field standing in for a farm. This is the other half.

It also answered a question nobody asked. The animation below was built to show the physics; what it showed instead was a wake standing 3.5 D upstream of the only turbine in its row — a defect in the framework's own coupling rather than in the pretrained model, and worth roughly twice the checkpoint's own disagreement in array loss. Every number here is measured after that repair, and the superseded record is kept beside the new one so the size of the correction stays checkable.

The array

The checkpoint chose the geometry

Poseidon-T is fixed at 128×128 and everything else follows from it. One macro-step must be one native lead of the trained model, and the freestream must land inside its trained velocity distribution — so a window spanning S rotor diameters forces both the macro-step (Δt = S/20) and the effective Reynolds number (Re = 1020/S). Choosing the window chooses the timestep and the physics and how much lateral room a wake has to recover in. Here the Reynolds band is spent to buy the geometry, and the turbine spacing is a whole number of window strides by construction.

Plan view, to scale — 11.0 × 7.5 rotor diameters

The flow

Streamwise velocity over 60 macro-steps, from a uniform freestream. Both runs use identical geometry, identical rotors, identical controls and the same macro-step; the only difference is which expert advances the fluid.

Where to look: the left third, upstream of R1. The classical solver holds it at freestream until the flow feels R1's blockage, which is the only thing that may slow it. At farm scale, 10.65 percentage points is the quantity wake steering is bought to move. A certificate that cannot see the difference between these two columns is not worth having.

Three honesties, without which this table should not be quoted. Neither column is validated against data — the classical solver is the declared referent, not ground truth. The one independent check does not rank them: inflow is sampled 0.25 D upstream, where momentum theory's 0.667 is a lower bound, so +16.4% against +24.3% bounds how much upstream induction each produces rather than which is right. And the two now agree closely where the physics is strongest and disagree where it is weakest: R2 sits in a developed wake and they are 4.6 pp apart, while R3 sits in the bypass flow beside one and there they differ in sign — the checkpoint says R3 gains 8.5% from its neighbour's blockage, the referent says it loses 4.3%.

And the number is robust where it needs to be. The referent's own viscosity is under-determined by 27× — the checkpoint behaves like a large-eddy model with a spectral cutoff, so its viscosity is not a number at all — and τ moves only 1.05× across that whole band, 11.58 to 11.03. Where it does not work

Six things this case study broke

Each carries its worklist number and its status. Real geometry is what made them visible: a schematic rotor hangs off any ring, so it never forces the bond question; a synthetic seam sits at zero, so it never forces the linearization question; a solver you can run at any resolution has a monolith, so it never forces the referent question. And a domain with no distinguished upstream never forces W98 — a wake has to have somewhere it is not allowed to be.

W98Closed

The composition ran two global operators one window at a time

Transport and pressure both belong to the whole domain, and the march bought both from the per-window API. Each is periodic on its window: a wake reaching a window's outflow edge re-entered that same window's inflow edge, one full circulation every 20 macro-steps, and a disk's elliptic response was smeared across its own 4 D box. It manufactured a 25% velocity deficit 3.5 D upstream of a lone turbine, where nothing causes one, and it was worth roughly twice the checkpoint's own disagreement in array loss. A wider halo does not touch it: giving the wrapped strip exactly zero weight in the partition of unity moves the deficit by 0.012. Nothing asserted its way to this — it was found by watching the animation above beside the referent's. The manufactured wake is smooth, bounded, physically shaped and in the right units, and a wake upstream of the only turbine is obvious in one second and invisible in a table.

W99Open

The body force is dropped silently

The expert wrapper applies force only inside its Galilean branch, so a caller that moves the translation up to the whole domain — which W98's repair must — gets a forward pass with no body force, no error and no warning. The same branch pins each window's output to zero mean, which deletes the momentum deficit a disk has just deposited. Both are one line; neither announces itself.

W93Closed

The halo declaration was 32× short

The record declares a two-cell domain of dependence. Poking a single delta at the seam measures 64 — the response is nonzero in all 128 seam cells at every amplitude tested, and a 16-cell overlap had been passing on the strength of the 2. A neural operator's receptive field is global by construction, which is a fact about the architecture and not about the physics, so the repair is not a bigger integer: the rule now declines to answer from the record and points at the measurement. This is the residual W98 could not remove: after the repair, about 5% of spurious upstream deficit survives, and that part belongs to the checkpoint rather than to the coupling.

W95Open

A learned expert cannot be its own referent

τ is defined against a tightly coupled reference pair. That pair cannot be built from the checkpoint: 65 of 96 Jacobian directions sit below the probe's own reproducibility, and Newton diverges from the first iteration under truncation and under damping. Three candidate mechanisms eliminated by measurement, the fourth unnamed. The classical solver is therefore not a convenience here — it is load-bearing.

W97Open

Rotor seams are blind by the cell Reynolds number

The normative mechanical effort carries the viscosity and an actuator disk's traction does not, so a field-to-lumped seam is one-sided by a factor of the cell Reynolds number before any expert is chosen. Its substitution certificate is blind by construction — it certifies any replacement of the fluid expert, including one that ignores its boundary data entirely, over a margin 81× that agent's own block.

W94Open

The actuator disk has no expressible bond

A disk is a surface across which the traction jumps, and a surface bond needs the two fluid subdomains to abut — a cut the framework refuses for a learned one-shot map. Under the overlapping decomposition the checkpoint does support, the disk sits inside the overlap and the coupling is two-way volumetric, which the port algebra has no bond for. The simplest, oldest, zero-parameter rotor model there is turns out to be inexpressible against a frozen operator, by two independent routes.

And one result that is hard rather than incidental: declare the elliptic content at the value the probe measures, and the compiler refuses the whole graph. That is the rule behaving correctly — it refuses to decompose any expert whose response is global, and a globally-receptive pretrained operator is nearly all of them.
