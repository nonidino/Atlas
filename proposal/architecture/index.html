Chart Operators: Learned Superelements for Domain Decomposition on Curved Geometry

Naunidh Singh
Draft of 1 October 2026

Research proposal. No network has been trained. Every number is either measured by a named script on classical solvers or untrained networks, or labelled as an estimate.

Abstract

Outline. One paragraph: the problem (learned local solvers inside a domain decomposition on non-rectangular geometry); the evidence that current learned solvers fail there (S1–S12); the design (topology to the decomposition, geometry to a classical certified chart, physics to a learned operator on the unit square that returns constraint modes, agreement to an energy Gram assembled by the host); the guarantee (symmetric positive semidefinite port matrices by construction, error quadratic and one-sided, Galerkin-optimal coupling); the measurements (coupling rounds, Gram against direct prediction, forward cost); the evaluation protocol.

1Introduction

Outline. The setting: a large simulation is split into subdomains that are solved separately and made to agree on their shared boundaries. The opportunity: replace the local solver by a trained network. The obstacle: such networks accept only rectangular grids, ignore the data their neighbours send, and change how fast the subdomains reach agreement. The idea, in four separations. Contributions (numbered, each with its status). Organisation of the paper.

1.1Contributions

Outline. (i) the chart rule with a discrete injectivity certificate and an envelope; (ii) the constraint-mode output with an analytic principal part; (iii) the host-side energy Gram and its three properties (Theorem, proved here); (iv) the measured cost of coupling strategies and of candidate backbones; (v) a pre-registered evaluation protocol; (vi) a counterexample to a published convergence step.

2Background

Outline. Written for a reader who knows linear algebra, multivariable calculus, real analysis and elliptic PDEs, and has not studied domain decomposition or machine learning. Every term used later is defined here.

2.1The model problem and its energy

Outline. Steady and implicit-step diffusion; weak form; energy inner product and energy norm; the discrete system $A u = f$ with $A$ symmetric positive definite; the Dirichlet principle (the solution minimises energy among functions with the same boundary values).

2.2Domain decomposition

Outline. Non-overlapping partition into subdomains; interface; the two transmission conditions (continuity of the solution and of the flux); the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map of a subdomain and its symmetry and positivity; the interface (Schur complement) equation; elimination of interior unknowns (static condensation).

2.3Iterative coupling: Schwarz methods and their acceleration

Outline. The alternating Schwarz method (each subdomain solved with its neighbours' latest values as boundary data); additive and multiplicative variants; Robin transmission conditions and optimised Schwarz methods; why the number of iterations grows with the number of subdomains, and the coarse space that removes this growth; Krylov acceleration (GMRES) and Anderson acceleration, defined.

2.4Substructuring with reduced interfaces

Outline. Constraint modes (the response to one interface mode with all others held at zero); port matrices; component mode synthesis and multiscale basis functions; the cost trade-off between iteration and assembly.

2.5Maps between domains

Outline. Charts onto the unit square; the Jacobian matrix and determinant; the pull-back of the diffusion equation; the Beltrami coefficient and dilatation; conformal, harmonic (Winslow) and transfinite maps; the injectivity theorem for harmonic maps onto convex domains.

2.6Neural operators

Outline. A network as a parametrised map between function spaces sampled on grids; training by minimising a loss over samples; convolutional and spectral architectures; the U-shaped multiscale layout; feature-wise linear modulation (FiLM) for scalar inputs; what "resolution-consistent" means.

3What the record shows

Outline. The measurement campaign on a frozen pretrained network (Poseidon-T) used as a local solver inside the project's coupled solvers. Table of S1–S12 with the measured failure and its consequence. The requirements they imply, and which section of this paper answers each.

4Architecture

Outline. Overview figure: domain, decomposition, charts, operator on the square, assembly.

4.1Topology: decomposition into four-sided pieces

Outline. Every piece a topological quadrilateral; the along/across harmonic coordinates; equal-value cuts and the conformal modulus; measured moduli on the s-channel and bend-3; figure of the s-channel decomposition.

4.2Geometry: certified charts

Outline. Geometry as anisotropy (the pull-back identity); the input fields $(\log J, \operatorname{Re}\mu, \operatorname{Im}\mu, \log\kappa)$; the corner trade-off (proposition); the chart rule; the discrete certificate (proposition); the envelope; figure comparing the three charts on the s-channel end piece; table of measured chart statistics.

4.3The expert's output: constraint modes

Outline. The canonical all-Dirichlet question; port modes (16 cosines per side); constraint modes and the particular field; the analytic principal part on the square; the bubble-multiplied correction; exact traces whatever the network returns; figure of principal parts.

4.4The backbone

Outline. U-shaped convolutional operator, odd/even extension, anti-aliased resampling, FiLM; the spectral ablation; size 1M with a {0.3M, 1M, 3M} sweep; reference grid 64, trained on 32/64/128.

4.5Ports, conservation and time

Outline. Interface data as cosine trace coefficients (tier 0) and Robin/wave coefficients (tier 2); the mortar projection from physical arc length; the conservative face-flux head; the time step entering as the Fourier number, with a declared range.

5Coupling and its guarantee

Outline. The host assembles each piece's energy Gram and solves one interface system. Theorem (Gram properties), with proof; Theorem (Galerkin optimality), with proof; Proposition (perturbation of the interface solve), with proof. The three-tier coupling stack. Tier 2: wave variables, the Cayley transform, the contraction condition, and the counterexample showing it cannot be weakened. Status of each statement: proved here on paper; none is machine-checked yet.

6Training

Outline. 7Cost and training time

8Evaluation protocol and gates
