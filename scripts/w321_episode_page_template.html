A rocket, marched

Six compressible gas blocks, a two-panel conducting airframe and a 3-DOF rigid body, advanced together for 0.145 s of flight at 25 km and Mach 3.5. Re-marched twice. The seams were rebuilt first: blocks that share a seam now share its node lines, and the coupler exchanges across each one by position, in each block's own gas model. That run failed its symmetry checks. The cause was a wall in the gas solver that passed mass, so this is the second re-march, with that fixed.

The airframe

The skin is 8 mm thick and 4.7 m long, in two panels either side of the axis, conducting along its length and loaded by the gas on both faces. Over the tank barrel (z < 0) the inner face borders a region no block models, and sees the wall the configuration declares there: adiabatic, at ambient pressure. Nose at the left.

The macro step is a declaration

This ran at Δtmacro = 5×10−3 s against the configuration's own 5×10−2. A tenfold reduction, and a conservative one: one chamber flow-through is about 6×10−3 s, so this sits at 0.83 flow-throughs. The multirate lag defect is first order in the exchange interval all the way to the configuration's own step, and the CS-11 bound holds there too, 2.28× loose. So the reduction cuts the coupling defect tenfold; it does not rescue a bound. (Tier 80 had the bound violated at 5×10−2 s, and Tier 84 found a knee at 1.67 flow-throughs. Both were the old isothermal wall's, and both went away when W334 re-derived them with the wall fixed.)

What these numbers can be trusted for

This run was re-run under a ledger that reads the face fluxes the solver itself applies, block by block and seam by seam (W336). Exact: every block conserves mass, momentum and energy to 10−13; the walls pass no mass; the skin's energy balance closes; and the re-run reproduces this page's run bitwise, on another machine. Right to about 1%: given the flow it receives, the nozzle's thrust is 0.989 of ideal planar-nozzle theory. Wrong, with a named cause: the injector delivers 17.7% more mass than the operating point declares, so the chamber runs at 1.13× its declared pressure (W337); the throat seam loses 3% of the mass flow every step, because blocks whose grids differ are joined by point interpolation (W340); the stationary walls do work, so 4.7% of the heat the gas loses at them never reaches the skin (W339); and the plume's seams do not conserve, because the plume block runs one gas model for exhaust and air (W341). Not known at this grid: every wall flux. The engine's wall heat doubles each time the wall cell halves, and at this resolution the air heats the skin in the wrong direction (W342). The skin temperatures and the drag shown here are not converged. The velocity gained carries an 8% error from the 5 ms coupling step by 60 ms. So this is a correct demonstration of the coupled machinery, and not yet a quantitative prediction of the vehicle.
